How to read this, and where it comes from

What "elementary" means here

DPI's ADM table reports one number per school and one per district; it does not report ADM by grade. DPI's location table classifies each school by grade span: E elementary, I elementary plus middle (K-8), M middle, H high, A all grades, T middle plus high. The default measure sums ADM at a district's E schools for each year, using that year's classification. The second measure adds I schools, which include grades 6 to 8. Elementary-grade students inside K-12 (A) schools, and in early colleges or special schools, are not captured by either.

The district-level E, M and H rows that exist in DPI's ADM table are the average school size in each category, not totals (CHCCS 2024-25: E = 411, which is 4,522 students in 11 schools). They are not used.

Pre-K

Pre-kindergarten is not in ADM, for any district or year. DPI's definition counts K-12 membership only, and the data agrees: DPI's separate pre-K enrollment table lists schools whose pre-K count exceeds their entire ADM (Sadie Saulter in Pitt County: ADM 32, pre-K 232), which could not happen if pre-K were inside ADM. So an elementary school labeled PK-5 contributes its K-5 students here.

Coverage

Alleghany and Watauga run only K-8 schools and have no E schools in any year; Hyde, Jackson and Yadkin lack E schools in a few years. Those units show hatched (no value) under the E-only measure and are covered by the E + K-8 measure. County figures sum the districts in the county plus, when selected, charter schools classified E or I and located there. Statewide in 2024-25, district E schools hold about 581,000 students and district K-8 schools about 41,000; charters add about 5,000 in E schools and 46,000 in K-8 schools, since many charters are K-8.

DPI notes that its 2024-25 final ADM may be higher than expected because of a transition to a new student information system; treat 2024-25 one-year changes with that in mind. Other caveats from tab 1 (boundary changes, federal and tribal districts, the pandemic year) apply here too, and the full list is in LIMITATIONS.md.

How to read this, and where it comes from

Measures

Private school students and private schools: self-reported to NC DNPE by conventional private schools, a point-in-time count each school year. School counts by county begin in 2007-08.

Home school students (est.) and home schools: DNPE counts registered home schools directly and estimates students from a sample of reporting schools. A home school is a household, not a student.

Non-public students: private plus home school. Non-public share divides that by all students counted in any sector (district ADM + charter ADM + private + home). Per 100 school-age children divides by the county's Census population aged 5 to 17.

Opportunity Scholarship recipients: students funded in 2024-25 by county of residence, new and returning, as of Feb. 3, 2025. Only one year of county data has been published, so this measure has no time series.

Views

Level is the raw measure. 1-year, 3-year average, cumulative since base and below peak are the same change metrics as tab 1. Share-type measures offer level and change-in-points since base.

Data

NC Department of Administration, Division of Non-Public Education: annual private school and home school statistical summaries, 2005-06 through 2025-26 (PDF and Word reports, parsed by county). The 2015-16 home school report is not on DNPE's site; that year is interpolated from its neighbors. Public ADM is NC DPI (tab 1). Population is the U.S. Census Bureau (tab 3). Vouchers: NCSEAA annual reports to the Joint Legislative Education Oversight Committee (2020-21, 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25) and the legislative staff table of 2024-25 recipients by county and income tier.

Limits

Private and home school numbers are self-reported and DNPE describes them as best-available estimates; reporting compliance rose after 2023 when it became a condition of receiving voucher funds, so part of the 2023-25 private school jump may be better reporting. Home school enrollment is a sample-based estimate, so single-year moves (the 2020-21 spike to 179,900 and the fall after it) are partly estimation noise. Students are counted where the school (or household) is located, not where the child would otherwise attend public school; Wake, Mecklenburg and Durham private schools draw from neighboring counties.

Source corrections. Two printed values were replaced by the average of their neighboring years because the reports' own totals and neighbors show them to be typos: Alexander County home schools in 2013-14 (printed as 3; 304 and 337 the years around it) and Alamance County home school students in 2022-23 (printed as 3,610, the same as Iredell's entry on the same line; the report's grand total is 1,828 below the sum of its rows). The 2005-06 private school report's grand total (92,867) is 200 above the sum of its own county rows; the county rows are used as printed.

Vouchers. The 2024-25 county table is dated Feb. 3, 2025 and sums to 79,775; the annual report's June 30 total is 80,472. Graham County's row could not be read from the table. NCSEAA's 2021-22 annual report was not located, so that year is missing from the statewide bars. 2025-26 non-public figures come from the current-year reports, which DNPE may revise.

How to read this, and where it comes from

Measures

School-age population (5-17): Census Bureau estimates of residents aged 5 to 17 as of July 1, matched to the school year that ends that June (the July 2024 estimate pairs with 2023-24), the same pairing the CHCCS notebook uses. The latest estimate is July 2024, so 2024-25 has no population value and shows as "no value"; the page opens on 2023-24. Total population: all residents. Public enrollment: district plus charter ADM for counties, district ADM for districts (tab 1).

Public students per 100 school-age children: enrollment divided by the 5-17 population. Above 100 is possible when a district draws students from outside its boundary or when the population estimate runs low. For districts the numerator excludes charter students, so a falling value can mean charter growth as much as private or home schooling.

Enrollment minus population gap: cumulative percent change in enrollment since the base year minus cumulative percent change in the 5-17 population. Negative means enrollment fell faster (or grew slower) than the child population.

Quadrant: the sign of each change since the base year, in four classes. "Kids up, enrollment down" is the one that points to families choosing other options.

Data

Counties: U.S. Census Bureau Population Estimates Program, county characteristics by age and sex, three vintages stitched together (2000-2010 intercensal, Vintage 2019, Vintage 2024). The intercensal file only has 5-year age groups, so 2005-2009 values for ages 5-17 are scaled from ages 5-19 using each county's own July 2010 ratio. Districts: U.S. Census Bureau Small Area Income and Poverty Estimates (SAIPE), school district estimates of total population and population aged 5-17, 2005 through 2024, matched to DPI districts through the Census district boundaries used on the maps.

Limits

Estimate seams. SAIPE district estimates before 2010 were built on 2000 Census geography and undercount fast-growing districts; Chapel Hill-Carrboro's 5-17 population steps up from about 9,600 to about 12,400 between 2008-09 and 2010-11 for that reason, not because 3,000 children arrived. Use a base year of 2010-11 or later for district comparisons. County estimates change vintage at 2010 and again at 2020, and the 2020 seam moves some small counties by 10% or more in one year (Duplin, Hyde, Gates). Treat 2018-19 to 2019-20 population changes with care.

Boundary change. Nash-Rocky Mount Schools split along the county line for 2024-25 (see tab 1). SAIPE's 2024 estimate adopted the new boundary, which under this pairing lands on 2023-24, one year before the student transfer shows in ADM; the Nash and Edgecombe gap values for 2023-24 are therefore not meaningful. The map still draws the 2023 Census shape.

Who is counted where. ADM counts students where they attend; population counts children where they live. Federal and tribal schools (Fort Bragg, Camp Lejeune, Cherokee Central) are not NC districts, so county capture rates in Cumberland, Onslow, Swain and Jackson run low and Swain County Schools' district-level rate runs above 100 (its resident count excludes the tribal district's area). Small city districts such as Elkin and Mount Airy enroll out-of-district transfers and can also exceed 100. Ages 5 to 17 is not exactly K-12: some 5-year-olds are not yet in kindergarten and some 18-year-olds are still enrolled. A full list of caveats is in LIMITATIONS.md.
